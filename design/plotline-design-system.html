Plotline Design system · v0.1 draft
Jeffway Electrical · AI takeoff

From tender pack to priced plot, without a scale rule.

Plotline is the working name for the Jeffway takeoff app. It reads a developer's tender pack, counts every symbol on every house type, and multiplies through the accommodation schedule. It then writes the counts into the Second Fix sheet the estimators already trust. The interface works like a drafting table: the drawing takes the full screen, the tools stay at the edges, and every number can be traced back to a mark on a sheet.

The story

The six jobs the app does

This is the process Lewis walked through on 17 July. The app's top bar shows these stages, so an estimator always knows where a tender stands.

01PackDrop in the tender pack. The app finds electrical sheets (the -E and -C drawings) and the accommodation schedule.
02LegendReads the symbol key from the sheet, or from a separate legend page.
03CountA local vision pass counts symbols in seconds. AI only checks the uncertain hits.
04ReviewThe estimator approves or rejects each mark from the keyboard. Nothing is counted without a decision.
05PlotsPer-house-type counts × plots from the accommodation schedule (e.g. Avon × 8).
06WorkbookFills the house-type tabs. Second Fix then sets First Fix cable and labour per item, and the Tender sheet calculates the rest.
Principles

How it should feel

The drawing is the hero

The canvas gets at least 70% of the screen. Panels collapse to an icon rail or hide completely ( [ and ] keys). Press F for focus mode, which hides everything except the drawing and the review dock.

Every number is traceable

Click any quantity in the bill of quantities to jump to those marks on the drawing. Click any mark to see which row it feeds. Each AI decision keeps its reason.

Keyboard first, mouse friendly

A approves, X rejects, J and K move to the next or previous mark, and ⌘K opens the command bar. Estimators review hundreds of marks, so each one should take a single keystroke.

Quiet chrome, loud state

Panels use graphite tones. Colour is kept for what needs a decision: needs review, rejected, AI-checked. Copper marks the one main action on screen.

Their language, not ours

Use the estimators' words: "Second Fix", "plots", "house type", "twin switched socket". Never "inference", "NCC" or "candidates" in the UI.

Calm motion

Panels slide in 220 ms with one easing curve. There is no bounce or decorative glow. Motion only explains where something went.

Colour

Graphite, copper, and four signals

The neutrals have a slight blue cast, like drafting film. Copper is the brand accent, taken from the cable every takeoff ends in, and it is used once per screen. Status colours are separate from it, so a copper button never reads as a warning.

Research

What we took from whom

Togal.AI · STACK · Kreo

The direct competitors: AI-first takeoff, cloud STACK, browser-based Kreo. Reviewers rank Togal and STACK as needing fewer clicks than Procore, and say Kreo's AI pass always needs a human check.

Take: speed comes from fewer clicks, and the human review step should be built in, not optional.

Bluebeam Revu

The tool subcontractors already mark up plans in. Estimators expect its markup and measurement conventions.

Take: familiar measuring and markup gestures, and an annotated PDF export.

Linear

Its 2024 redesign cut visual noise and generated light and dark themes in LCH from a base, an accent and a contrast level.

Take: themes generated from tokens, a keyboard-first workflow, quiet chrome.

Figma UI3

Resizable, collapsible panels and a canvas-first layout. Its floating panels were reverted after power users said the canvas felt smaller.

Take: dock the panels and float only the review bar. Give users a single key to hide all the UI.
